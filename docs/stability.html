3 · Stability analysis · Sec. V

Why the error stays bounded

Section V shows that the closed loop of the heat equation, the ergodic controller, and the standoff controller drives \norm{e(\cdot,t)}_{L^2(\ROI)} to a bounded neighbourhood of zero. The argument has three steps. The workspace animates them on the one-dimensional loop of Fig. 4.

The 1D loop of Fig. 4

On \Omega=[0,1] with \ROI=[0.2,0.6], the source heats every point of \ROI at once, in proportion to the deficit, and the gate is the only state left to the controller:

\qin(\vx,t)=g(t)\,k_p\,e_+(\vx,t),\qquad \dot g=k_I\,\ebar,\quad g\in[0,1]

The run has five 20 s regimes: start-up, a hot disturbance \Dext=+18 °C/s, recovery, a cold disturbance -18 °C/s, and recovery. The step of \Ts at x=0.3 is sharper than diffusion can hold.

Step 1 · Reduction to the mean error

The error splits into its spatial mean and a zero-mean part, e=\ebar+e^\perp:

\norm{e}_{L^2}^2=|\ROI|\,\ebar^2+\norm{e^\perp}_{L^2}^2

Through the Poincaré inequality, diffusion damps e^\perp to a bounded set, \norm{e^\perp}\le e^\perp_{\max}, which includes a floor e^\perp_{\mathrm{reach}} for targets with gradients that diffusion cannot match. Hence

\norm{e}_{L^2}^2\le|\ROI|\,\ebar^2+(e^\perp_{\max})^2,

and the rest of the analysis concerns the scalar \ebar.

Step 2 · Ideal convergence of the gate

For a fixed gate, the heat equation has a unique stable equilibrium T_g, with mean error \ess(g)=\langle\Ts-T_g\rangle (28). More gate means more heating, so (Lemma 1)

g_2>g_1\ \Rightarrow\ \ess(g_2)<\ess(g_1),

with a zero at g^\star\in(0,1) and slope -\ess'\ge m_e>0 (Assumption 1). With the Lyapunov function

V(g)=-\int_{g^\star}^{g}\ess(s)\,ds\ \ge0,\qquad \dot V=-k_I\,\ess(g)^2\le0,

the gate converges to g^\star and \ebar\to0 for any k_I>0 (Theorem 1). Each disturbance regime has its own map and its own g^\star_r.

Step 3 · Ultimate boundedness (GUUB)

With the real single moving source and bounded disturbances, the mean error differs from the ideal map by a bounded residual:

\ebar(t)=\ess\big(g(t)\big)+\Delta(t),\qquad |\Delta(t)|\le\bar\Delta,

where \bar\Delta grows with the coverage residual, the standoff variation, and the disturbances. The same V decreases whenever |\ess(g)|>\bar\Delta (40), so after a finite time (Theorem 2)

|\ess(g)|\le\bar\Delta,\qquad |g-g^\star|\le\bar\Delta/m_e,\qquad |\ebar|\le2\bar\Delta.

In the workspace, the sweep period \tau_{\mathrm{erg}} replaces the distributed heating by a source that sweeps \ROI with the same time-averaged heating (33); the time-scale separation \tau_{\mathrm{erg}}\ll\tau_{\mathrm{th}} keeps \bar\Delta small.

Discrete-time gain bound (Sec. V-D)

g_{k+1}=\Pi_{[0,1]}\big(g_k+k_I\,\Delta t\,\ebar_k\big)

Linearized near g^\star, stability and non-oscillatory convergence require

k_I<\frac{2}{\Delta t\,|\ess'(g^\star)|},\qquad k_I<\frac{1}{\Delta t\,|\ess'(g^\star)|}.

In continuous time any k_I>0 converges; the explicit-Euler loop does not. Since \ess' depends on the unknown thermal properties, k_I is chosen small.

Try this

  1. Play the default run (discrete, k_I=0.1) and follow g(t) to each g^\star_r; V(g(t)) steps up at every regime change and then decays.
  2. In Step 1, compare |\ROI|^{1/2}|\ebar|, which the gate drives to zero, with \norm{e^\perp}, which stays bounded; without disturbances, it stays at the floor set by the step of \Ts.
  3. In Step 2, watch the state slide down V(g). Move the start gate g(0): every start reaches the same g^\star.
  4. In Step 3, switch the disturbances off and set the sweep period to \tau_{\mathrm{erg}}=0.2 s, then 0.5 s: g no longer converges to g^\star but settles in a band, within the bounds (37)–(38). As \tau_{\mathrm{erg}} approaches \tau_{\mathrm{th}}\approx1.8 s, the residual grows and the gate saturates at 1: the time-scale separation no longer holds.
  5. Set k_I=20: the discrete loop sustains a limit cycle, the continuous loop converges. Compare the four cases reproduces the bottom row of Fig. 4.

Diffusion bounds the shape of the error, the monotone map \ess(g) and the integral law drive its mean to zero, and the coverage and disturbance residuals only enlarge the final set: the error is ultimately bounded, not divergent.
