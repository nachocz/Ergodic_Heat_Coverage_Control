The temperature regulation problem

A single heat source, moved by a robot over a surface, has to bring the temperature field to a target distribution on a region of interest. This page introduces the quantities of the problem; in the workspace, you move the heat source by hand.

Surface and region of interest

The surface is a 2D manifold parametrized over \Omega=[0,1]^2 and embedded in the workspace by \vs:\Omega\to\R^3. The task is defined on \ROI\subseteq\Omega, given by its indicator \chiROI. Here, as in the code, \ROI is the set where the target is above ambient:

\ROI=\{\vx\in\Omega:\ \Ts(\vx)>\Tamb\}.

Its boundary \partial\ROI is the dotted teal line in every view.

Temperature, target, and error

e(\vx,t)=\Ts(\vx)-T(\vx,t),\qquad \vx\in\ROI.

The objective is to minimize \norm{e(\cdot,t)}_{L^2(\ROI)}. The source can only add heat, so the controller separates the heat deficit

e_+(\vx,t)=\max\{e(\vx,t),0\}\ \text{on}\ \ROI,\quad 0\ \text{elsewhere},

(red in the error map) from the over-heated regions (blue).

Heat equation

\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot\big(\alpha(\vx)\nabla T\big)+\lambda(\vx)\big(\qin-\qloss(T)\big)

\alpha=\kappa/(c\rho) is the diffusivity, \lambda=1/(c\rho) the thermal coupling, and \qloss=h\,(T-\Tamb) the loss to the environment, with Neumann boundaries. These quantities are unknown to the controller: the model is used only to simulate the surface and in the stability analysis.

Actuation

\qin(\vx,t)=P\,\eta\big(d(t)\big)\,\K\big(\vx;\vp(t),d(t)\big),\qquad \int_\Omega\K\,d\vx=1
\Qin(t)=\int_{\ROI}\qin\,d\vx=P\,\eta\big(d(t)\big)

The source has a constant power P. The efficiency \eta(d) decreases with the standoff d and vanishes at \dmax, while the width \sigma(d) of the Gaussian kernel \K grows with d. The standoff therefore sets both the injected power and its spread.

Robot workspace

\vR(t)=\vR_s\big(\vp(t)\big)=[\vt_1,\ \vt_2,\ \vn]\in SO(3)
\vv_s=\begin{bmatrix}\vu_\tau\\u_n\end{bmatrix},\quad \dot{\vp}=\vu_\tau,\quad \dot d=u_n,\quad \vv_{\mathrm{global}}=\vR_s\vv_s

The end effector stays aligned with the surface frame (\vt_1 red, \vt_2 green, \vn blue in the 3D view) at \vr=\vs(\vp)+d\,\vn. The tangential input \vu_\tau moves the focal point \vp, and the normal input u_n changes the standoff d. Sections III and IV define the two controllers (control demo).

Try this

  1. Press and drag on the large map to move the focal point \vp while the source heats. The heat deficit e_+ shrinks where you pass, and diffusion spreads the heat beyond the footprint.
  2. Scroll on the map, or use the slider, to change d, and compare \eta(d) and \sigma(d) in the actuation plot: close to the surface, the source heats a small spot strongly; at \dmax it does not heat.
  3. Over-heat a region: e becomes negative (blue). Only losses and diffusion lower T; there is no active cooling.
  4. Switch the surface to the scanned sole and follow the frame \vR_s and the standoff along \vn in the 3D view.
  5. Click a row of the notation table to show that quantity on the large map.

To heat a whole distribution with one moving source, the controller must decide where the source goes (\vp, Sec. III) and how much heat it delivers (d, Sec. IV), using only the measured error.
